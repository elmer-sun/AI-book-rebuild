From Examples 1.3.2 and 1.3.3 it can be seen that we can often find a matrix
representation of a group without specifying an underlying vector space and a choice
of basis. This is because an abstract group has homomorphisms that are matrix
groups, and are therefore matrix representations. However, as far as we are con-
cerned, the way representations arise out of Definition 1.3.4, as illustrated by Example
1.3.1, seems to be more natural. This is because all the groups in which we are in-
terested have geometrical realizations, and their representations are most easily con-
structed by considering the action of their elements within these realizations on
appropriate vector spaces.
